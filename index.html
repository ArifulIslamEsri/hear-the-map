Hear the Map

Explore the world by ear. Put on headphones: left and right matter.

  • Pitch is height. Low notes are lowlands, high notes are mountains.
  • Waves are water. At or below sea level you hear a soft wash.
  • Pings are earthquakes. Closer ones echo back first; louder means stronger; brighter means shallower.

About Hear the Map

What it is

A map you can explore with your ears. Height becomes pitch, water becomes a soft wash, left and right follow the map, and recent earthquakes answer back like sonar. Every sound also comes with words for screen readers.

Why it matters

About 43 million people worldwide are blind, and 295 million more have moderate or severe vision impairment. For them, a web map is usually a blank rectangle with a one-line description. A description tells you a map exists. Sound lets you explore it yourself: find the valley, follow a ridge, hear where the land meets the sea.

What's new

Map sonification has been tried before, in research prototypes and in specialist map viewers. This project shows how to do it directly inside the ArcGIS Maps SDK for JavaScript, with live terrain and live data, as open source that any developer can add to their own maps. Once the map settles, listening makes no network requests at all.
